Automation & Systems Architecture

Order & Inventory Automation

Connecting Shopify, n8n, and Airtable to automate customer matching, order creation, unit-level records, and sequential inventory assignment.

RoleSystems designer & implementer
BusinessLimited-edition ecommerce
WorkflowShopify → n8n → Airtable
ComplexityMulti-item and multi-quantity orders

The challenge

What needed to change

Each physical product needed its own numbered inventory record, even when a customer bought multiple units in a single Shopify line item. The workflow also had to match or create customers, preserve order relationships, select the next available product item, and prevent duplicate allocation.

My role

What I owned

I designed the Airtable data model, mapped Shopify order data, built the n8n logic, troubleshot linked-record and quantity handling, and validated the full workflow using multi-unit test orders.

The approach

How I worked through it

01

Normalize the order

Prepared Shopify order data so customer, order, line-item, and quantity information could move reliably through the automation.

02

Match the customer

Used email first with a normalized phone fallback to find or create the correct Airtable customer.

03

Expand by quantity

Converted each Shopify line item into one unit-level record per purchased product.

04

Allocate inventory

Searched for the first available product item by SKU, linked it to the order item, and marked it unavailable.

System flow

Shopify order→n8n trigger→Customer match→Airtable order→Expand quantity→Allocate inventory

Results

What changed

One Airtable order item created per physical unit
Sequential inventory allocation validated
Multi-quantity orders handled correctly
Manual assignment removed from the order workflow
Clear relational links across customers, orders, order items, and product items

Tools & capabilities

Shopifyn8nAirtableJavaScriptJSONREST conceptsData modeling

What I learned

The most important design decision was separating a Shopify line item from a physical unit. Once quantity was expanded into unit-level records, inventory allocation became reliable and auditable.
