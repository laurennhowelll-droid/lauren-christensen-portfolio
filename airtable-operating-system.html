Data Architecture & Operations

Airtable Business Operating System

Structuring Airtable as a connected source of truth for customers, orders, inventory, production, refunds, repairs, insurance, and reporting.

RoleSystem architect
FocusRelational business operations
UsersLeadership and cross-functional teams
OutcomeCentralized operational visibility

The challenge

What needed to change

Operational information existed across ecommerce, customer communication, production workflows, shipping, and spreadsheets. Teams needed a shared system that could preserve relationships between records while making day-to-day work easier.

My role

What I owned

I designed table relationships, linked records, views, formulas, automations, reporting structures, and operational workflows based on how each department actually used the information.

The approach

How I worked through it

01

Model relationships

Separated customers, orders, order items, inventory, products, and operational events into connected records.

02

Design for users

Created views and interfaces around real team responsibilities instead of forcing every department into the same layout.

03

Automate handoffs

Reduced re-entry by moving data between ecommerce, operational, and reporting workflows.

04

Create visibility

Built reporting and management views that surfaced exceptions, workload, financial impact, and customer outcomes.

Relational structure

Customers↔Orders↔Order items↔Inventory↔Production↔Reporting

Results

What changed

Centralized operational data
Improved production and order visibility
Stronger record relationships
More consistent reporting
Scalable foundation for additional automations

Tools & capabilities

AirtableLinked recordsFormulasAutomationsInterfacesn8nShopify

What I learned

A business operating system should reflect how the company works—not how a spreadsheet is traditionally organized. Relational design made both automation and reporting significantly stronger.
